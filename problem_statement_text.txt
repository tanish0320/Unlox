Blu Visual Teacher Hackathon — Problem
Statement
 Oct 28, 2026  ·
In 24 hours, build an offline system that turns a typed concept into a short, narrated,
animated lesson in English and at least two Indian languages.
Why this matters
Concepts like a loop in code, a chemical reaction or a law of physics are easier to grasp
when you see them move step by step and hear them explained in your own language.
Today, one short explainer video like that takes a teacher hours of scripting, drawing,
recording and editing.
Your job is to build Blu Visual Teacher: a system that makes that video in minutes, on a
laptop, without the internet.
Your challenge
Build a local-first engine that takes a concept (for example, "How does a Python loop
work?") and produces a 30–90 second visual lesson: animated scenes, on-screen
captions and a voice-over in the learner's chosen language — English plus at least two
Indian languages such as Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali or Marathi. The lesson must play in
the browser and export as an MP4.
You are building a video engine, not a video-generating AI model. A small local language
model writes the lesson plan as structured data. Your own code turns that data into
animated diagrams, code walkthroughs and narration. Correct, clear teaching matters
more than cinematic visuals.
Ground rules
These are hard rules. Breaking one makes your team ineligible for the main prize.
No external AI APIs. No OpenAI, Gemini, Claude, ElevenLabs, Google Translate, or
Google or Microsoft cloud voices. Every model runs on your own machine.
Laptops only. Plan for a CPU-only laptop with 8–16 GB of RAM. A GPU can speed things
up, but your demo must also work without one.
Works offline. Once models and packages are downloaded, the whole pipeline must
run with the internet switched off.

Key design rule: the voice-over sets each scene's length. Generate the narration audio
first, then make each scene last as long as its audio plus a short pause. If you hard-code
scene durations, your audio and visuals will drift apart.
Language tip: small local models write weaker Hindi or Tamil than English. A reliable
approach is to plan the lesson in English, then translate the narration and captions with a
local translation model (for example, AI4Bharat's IndicTrans2). Keep code, numbers and
technical terms in English where students would say them that way.
Input and output
Input: a topic (free text), a learner level, a language code (for example en, hi, ta, te, bn, mr)
and a target length in seconds.
Lesson plan (what your LLM returns): JSON that matches a schema you define and
validate. Use the example below as a starting point; you may add scene types and fields.

Output: the lesson playing in the browser, plus a downloadable MP4 (1280×720, H.264

Reference video: at kickoff we will play a 29-second sample lesson on Python loops. It

Bonus points
Bonus points count only once all five core stages work.
More languages: each Indian language beyond the required two, and a switch that re-
voices a finished lesson in another language without planning it again.
Voice choice: a male or female voice, and a speed control.
More scene types: a graph or chart, a timeline, a labelled illustration, a quiz question at
the end.
Edit before render: let a teacher change a scene's narration or content, then
regenerate only that scene.
Reuse: cache finished lessons by topic, language and version so the same lesson is
never generated twice.
Speed: show how long each stage takes on your weakest laptop.
What to submit
Submit by the end of hour 24:
1. Code repository with a README covering setup steps, the models and voices you
used and their licences, and the laptop specs you tested on.
2. Two generated MP4 lessons: one on a topic the judges give you on the day, and one on
a topic of your choice. Each must be in English and in one Indian language.
3. A 5-minute live demo: type a new topic and language the judges call out, generate the
lesson live, and play it. Then 3 minutes of questions.

Before you arrive
Rules
All code must be written during the 24 hours. Open-source libraries and pre-trained
models are allowed.
Download pre-trained models and packages before the event. Venue Wi-Fi may be
slow, and training your own foundation model is out of scope.
Open source does not always mean free for any use. Check each model's weights, code
and voice licences separately.
Download
Our advice: get the whole chain working on one hard-coded lesson first, then connect the
language model. A simple lesson that plays end to end beats a clever model with no video.
Ollama, plus one small instruction model (around 3B parameters for 8–16 GB laptops)
Local text-to-speech for every language you plan to support. Kokoro ( kokoro-onnx )
runs on CPU and covers Indian-accented English and Hindi (voices hm_omega , hm_psi ,
hf_alpha , hf_beta ). For other Indian languages, try AI4Bharat's open Indic voice
models (such as Indic Parler-TTS) and test their speed on your laptop.
A local translation model for Indian languages, such as AI4Bharat's IndicTrans2
Noto Sans fonts for each Indian script you support, so captions and the MP4 show text
correctly
FFmpeg
Node.js and Python with your packages already installed (for example React, FastAPI,
